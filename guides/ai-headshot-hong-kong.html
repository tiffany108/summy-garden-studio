AI headshots in Hong Kong

A studio session in Central runs HK$1,500–4,000 and takes a fortnight. Here is what the AI alternative actually delivers, and where it still falls short.

What a professional headshot costs in Hong Kong

A traditional corporate headshot session in Hong Kong typically runs between HK$1,500 and HK$4,000 for an individual sitting. That usually covers an hour in-studio, a handful of retouched finals, and a turnaround of one to two weeks. For a graduate applying to twenty firms, or a professional refreshing a LinkedIn profile, that is a significant outlay for a photo that will be replaced within a few years.

AI headshots occupy a different bracket entirely — typically HK$180–500 for a full set. The trade-off is not simply price. A photographer directs you, adjusts your posture, and captures genuine expression. AI works from photos you already have.

What Hong Kong recruiters actually look for

Across banking, law, and the Big Four in Hong Kong, expectations are conservative and consistent:

  • Plain or softly blurred background. Office, studio grey, or neutral. Avoid holiday backdrops.
  • Business formal for finance and law. Navy or charcoal suit, white or light blue shirt. Smart casual is acceptable in tech and creative fields.
  • Head and shoulders framing. Your face should occupy a large part of the frame.
  • A natural, composed expression. A slight smile reads as approachable; a broad grin can read as informal for finance roles.
  • Recent and recognisable. If you would not be identified at reception from the photo, it is doing you harm.
A point worth being honest about: AI headshots are widely accepted for LinkedIn, CVs and internal directories. They are not appropriate for identity documents, visa applications, or the HKID — those require genuine photographs taken to specification.

Dressing appropriately without a Western default

Most international AI headshot tools were built around Western business wear, which leaves gaps for candidates in Hong Kong and the wider region. A good tool should offer the cheongsam, mandarin-collar suits, and modest options such as a hijab worn with a blazer — not as novelties, but as ordinary professional choices sitting alongside a navy suit.

How to get a result that actually looks like you

This is where most people go wrong. The single biggest factor in likeness is the quality of what you upload.

  1. Upload around eight photos. Three is a workable minimum, but more gives the system a real choice of angles.
  2. Vary the angle. Straight on, slightly turned, smiling, neutral.
  3. Use good light. Daylight near a window beats overhead office lighting.
  4. Fill the frame with your face. A group photo cropped down carries too little detail.
  5. Skip heavy filters and sunglasses. Anything that obscures your features works against you.

When you should still hire a photographer

If you are a partner, founder, or public-facing executive whose photograph will appear on a company website beside colleagues shot by the same photographer, hire the photographer. Consistency matters more than cost at that level. For everyone else — graduates, job changers, freelancers, anyone who simply needs a decent professional photo this week — the economics are hard to argue with.

Business formal for finance, law and professional services: a navy or charcoal suit with a white or light blue shirt. Smart casual is acceptable in tech and creative industries.
