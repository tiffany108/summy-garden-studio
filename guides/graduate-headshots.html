Headshots for graduates

You need a professional photo for applications, you do not have a professional wardrobe, and you certainly do not have a studio budget. This is the realistic route.

Do you actually need one?

It depends where you are applying. In much of Asia and continental Europe, a photograph on the CV is conventional and its absence is noticed. In the UK, the US and Canada, photographs are usually omitted from the CV itself to avoid bias — but your LinkedIn profile still needs one, and recruiters check it.

So the practical answer for nearly everyone: yes, you need one good photo. Whether it goes on the CV depends on the market.

What to wear when you do not own a suit

You do not need to buy one. What reads as professional in a headshot is mostly visible from the chest up:

  • A plain shirt or blouse in white, light blue, or another quiet colour
  • A simple knit or a blazer over a plain top
  • Solid colours rather than busy patterns, which distract at thumbnail size
  • Nothing with visible branding or slogans

If you are using an AI headshot tool, the clothing is generated — so what you wear in the photos you upload does not matter much. What matters is that your face is clear and well lit.

Taking usable source photos on a phone

  1. Stand facing a window in daylight, with the light on your face rather than behind you.
  2. Ask someone to take them from about a metre away at eye level — arm's-length selfies distort facial proportions.
  3. Take around eight, varying the angle slightly and alternating between a small smile and a neutral expression.
  4. Keep your face large in the frame, roughly head and shoulders.
  5. Skip beauty filters entirely. They remove exactly the detail that makes the result look like you.
Worth knowing: the quality of your uploads sets the ceiling on the result. No tool can invent facial detail that was not in the photo you gave it — which is why a well-lit phone photo beats a dark, distant one every time.

One photo, used everywhere

Use the same headshot across your LinkedIn profile, CV where appropriate, university careers portal, and any application forms. Consistency helps people remember you across a long recruitment process, and it costs nothing.

What to avoid

  • Graduation photos in cap and gown for corporate applications — save those for family
  • Cropped photos from nights out, however good you look in them
  • Anything more than two or three years old
  • Heavily edited images that no longer resemble you at interview
